## Week 2: at Buffalo (L 31–41)

Detroit was beaten clearly in Buffalo — down 27–10 at half and never within one score after a
late push to 34–24. The Lions were out-gained by 91 and gave up 41, the **second straight week
surrendering 30-plus** (30 to New Orleans, now 41 to Buffalo). The front actually played well —
Deone Walker and Aidan Hutchinson generated heavy pressure — but the back seven got carved up:
linebacker and safety coverage grades landed in the **20s–30s** (Jack Campbell, Devin White,
Avonte Maddox), and Josh Allen exploited the middle at will.

## Week 1: vs New Orleans (W 31–30, OT)

Detroit survived, not thrived — a 31–30 overtime win in which it blew a 21–0 lead and needed
a goal-line stop on a two-point try to beat a Saints team missing Alvin Kamara and starting a
second-year quarterback. The offense flashed early then stalled; the depleted secondary gave
up 30. The rating holds: one uneven win, the elite skill group intact, and the defensive
softness is the safety situation already baked into the number. New concern up front — **LG
Christian Mahogany** hurt his **right knee** and was carted off in the first quarter (Ben
Bartch replaced him); timeline TBD.

## The case for a top offense, a dinged defense

Detroit's rating is offense-led — a good quarterback fronting one of the league's best skill groups — but the defense takes a real hit at safety to open the season.

## Quarterback

Jared Goff (+2.0) is an above-average grade — a precise, high-volume passer perfectly fit to this scheme.

## What moved the number

- **Offense (+1.0):** elite — Gibbs, St. Brown, and Jameson Williams headline, behind a Sewell-anchored line. Pacheco (offseason RB add) is on IR, but Gibbs is the workhorse, so the depth loss is minor.
- **Defense (preseason −1.0; now −1.5 after Week 2 — see below):** cut from 0.0. **Both All-Pro safeties open the season out** — **Brian Branch** on PUP with a ruptured Achilles (from last December) and **Kerby Joseph** with a chronic knee that may cost him the season (latest reports have him headed to IR). That's a gutting blow at the position, layered on the offseason losses of DT DJ Reader and LB Anzalone. Aidan Hutchinson returning healthy at edge is the anchor that keeps this from cratering.

## What changed — Week 1 2026

- **Nothing moved (2.0 held).** An uneven OT win over a shorthanded Saints team isn't a
  rating signal — the skill group is intact and the defensive softness is the already-priced
  safety absence. Watching LG Christian Mahogany's knee (carted off Wk1).

## What changed — Week 2 2026

- **Defense cut to −1.5 (from −1.0).** This move is **not** a further discount for the injured
  safeties — that was already priced into the −1.0. It's about the players **on the field**:
  through two weeks the current unit has **demonstrated it is one of the worst defenses in the
  league**, giving up 30-plus in back-to-back games and getting carved up in coverage. There is
  real talent on the defensive line (Hutchinson, Walker), but **as a whole the group has proven
  bottom-tier**, and the rating now reflects that on-field reality rather than a projection.
  Offense unchanged — this is a defense problem.

## Bottom line

The offense is a top-tier unit and keeps Detroit a contender, but the defense has played its
way to a bottom-tier grade — the swing factor on this rating. The safeties returning is the
path back up, but the current group has to prove it can be more than one of the NFL's worst.
