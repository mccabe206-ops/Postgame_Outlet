More stable weights did not improve the predictions

Research result · September 14, 2026 · Experimental

We tested a change to how PGO balances its quarterback and team sack-avoidance measurements. Both partly describe the same plays. The test made it harder for their weights to diverge while keeping both inputs.

The candidate did not pass our accuracy requirements. PGO keeps its published model. The fitted weights became more stable across seasons, but the average prediction error was slightly higher.

What the error means

If PGO predicts a team will win by 4 and it wins by 7, the margin error is 3 NFL points. We averaged that absolute difference across the same games for both versions. This test evaluated winning margins. It did not test sportsbook covers, confidence pool points or projected total scores.

What we can conclude

This particular weight change did not earn a place in the public predictions. Keeping a failed test visible helps explain how PGO evaluates proposed improvements. This result does not establish the best weights or rule out other explanations for a team's ranking.

Each evaluation fit used seasons before its test season, covering 2018–2025. We have studied these historical games before, so they are not untouched evidence. Historical source timing and expected-quarterback limitations also remain unresolved. No 2026 game outcomes entered this experiment.

Technical details and retained evidence

The fixed candidate applied four times the original penalty to the disagreement direction of the standardized QB and team sack-avoidance values. Their common direction retained its original penalty. It preserved all other inputs, missing-data flags, saved training preprocessing, fold memberships and optimizer settings.

Exactly eight evaluation fits and one separate final historical fit ran once. The final fit is archived and has not issued any public predictions. The primary comparison was the saved postseason model; the regular-season corrected model, PGO v0 and training-fold constant were retained as contextual references.

The uncertainty range came from 10,000 resamples of whole seasons, preserving paired games. Measured coefficient drift decreased from 0.157450 to 0.085437. These are algebraic weight diagnostics, not player grades or causal football effects.
