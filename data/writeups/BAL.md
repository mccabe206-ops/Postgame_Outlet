## Week 3: vs Dallas in Rio de Janeiro (W 34–31)

A coin flip in the Maracanã, won on the last snap. Tyler Loop hit a **56-yard field goal as
time expired**, seconds after Dallas had tied it at 31. The box says these were two even teams.
The expected-points model had Dallas by about a point, so this was no statement win. What
Baltimore did better was **not beat itself**: zero turnovers and 4-of-6 in the red zone.
**Lamar Jackson closed** this time. He went 15-of-20 for 186 yards, 2 TD and 0 INT (94.0 QBR,
136.7 rating, No. 1 among the week's starters), added 50 rushing yards, and led the go-ahead
touchdown drive before getting Loop into range. Derrick Henry ground out 89 yards and two
scores. **Zay Flowers** was back from his hamstring (5 catches, 84 yards), and **Nnamdi
Madubuike**, in his return from neck surgery, was the best defender on the field.

The cost came up front. Baltimore played without **LT Ronnie Stanley (toe)**. Then it **lost
both centers in the second half**: starter **Jovaughn Gwyn** was carted off with an **ankle
injury**, and his replacement **Ethan Pocic** left with a **knee injury**, which pushed rookie
guard Vega Ioane to center. There's no update on either yet, and the team may need to add a
center. On defense, Dallas's receivers moved the ball (415 yards, 31 points), and Marlon
Humphrey had a rough night against Lamb and Pickens.

- **Lamar 4.0 → 4.5 (+0.5):** the Week 2 cut was for not finishing, and he finished. It was a
  clean, efficient game with two late scoring drives and no giveaways.
- **Offense held +0.8:** Flowers' return (the room we'd left in Week 1) is offset by the
  interior-line hits: both centers were hurt in-game, and Stanley is out.
- **Defense held +0.5:** Madubuike is healthy and dominant, a real plus, but the unit allowed 415
  yards and 31 points. It needs to show it over a few weeks before it climbs.
- **New total 5.3 → 5.8.**

Baltimore lost outright at home as an ~8.5-point favorite to New Orleans — the kind of result
that isn't acceptable at this price. The Ravens led 14–6 at the half on 223 yards, then went
quiet: 64 yards and 3 points over the final two quarters while the Saints hung 18. The box
backs the disappointment — Baltimore left points on the floor (roughly 4 under its expected
total) and the model already saw this as a near-even game it slightly favored New Orleans to
win. Lamar Jackson didn't close it out (21-of-31, 235, 1 TD, 1 INT), and the turnover was a
terrible game-sealing INT at the end. A collective flop — offense stalled after halftime, and
a defense priced near the top let a thin, banged-up group win in Baltimore.

- **Lamar 4.5 → 4.0 (-0.5):** terrible game-sealing INT at the end and didn't close (21/31,

## Week 1: at Indianapolis (W 41–23)

Baltimore rolled — 41–23 at Indianapolis, 28 unanswered after the Colts' opening drive, a
506–251 yardage edge. Derrick Henry ran for 144 and three touchdowns, Lamar Jackson was in
full control, and the pass rush hit Daniel Jones nine times. It played like the elite rating,
and the offense earns a small bump for it — held modest because **Zay Flowers** (150 receiving
yards) left with a hamstring and will likely miss around a week. Also dinged: rookie WR
**Ja'Kobi Lane** (wrist, possibly a longer absence) and corners **T.J. Tampa Jr.** (knee) and
**Marlon Humphrey** (right leg).

The one caveat holding it at 1.0 rather than higher is **Justin Madubuike recovering from neck surgery** (his status is genuinely uncertain — possibly IR), which thins the interior rush to start.

## Bottom line

Elite quarterback, above-average offense, and a defense the ratings expect to climb under Minter's return to the Macdonald scheme. If Madubuike gets healthy, this is one of the most complete rosters in the league.
