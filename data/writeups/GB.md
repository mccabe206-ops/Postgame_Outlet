## The case for an ascending team with a delayed defense

Green Bay grades above the line — a rising quarterback and a functional offense — with a defense that's holding a placeholder grade until its marquee addition is healthy.

## Quarterback

Jordan Love (+1.0) is an above-average, still-ascending starter. The arm talent and flashes of top-tier play are there; the number stops short of elite because the consistency and durability haven't fully arrived. This is a quarterback the model likes and expects to keep climbing, not one it's ready to price with the game's best yet.

## What moved the number

- **Offense (+0.5):** the backfield news is good — **Josh Jacobs won his Commissioner's Exempt-List appeal and plays Week 1** (the underlying conduct case is still pending, a real future risk, but he's available now), and **Tucker Kraft is back full-go from an ACL** to give Love a genuine tight-end weapon. The receiver room is young and unsettled after losing Doubs and Wicks — Golden, Watson, and Reed have to prove they're enough. A solid, above-average group carried by Jacobs and the run game.
- **Defense (−0.5):** this grade is deliberately conservative and **has real upside on a delay**. Green Bay added **Micah Parsons — but he's on PUP recovering from a torn ACL and misses roughly the first four games** (a genuine game-changer once he's back, targeted around Week 5). Until then, **Hargrave and Franklin anchor the interior and Xavier McKinney is an excellent safety**, but with Gary gone the edge is thin early. The number reflects the September version of this defense, not the one Parsons joins.

## Bottom line

An ascending quarterback and a run-first offense keep Green Bay above the line now, and the defense is a midseason story waiting to happen — the moment Parsons is on the field, this rating should climb. The early-season question is whether the young receivers and a thin edge hold up until the cavalry arrives.
