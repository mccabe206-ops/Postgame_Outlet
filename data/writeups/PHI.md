## Week 4: vs L.A. Rams (L 20–24)

A **20–10 halftime lead couldn't hide how badly Philadelphia was out-played.** The Rams **out-gained the
Eagles 415–172**. Philadelphia went **0-for-12 on third down**, threw for **68 net yards**, and managed
**67 yards and no points after halftime**. The box-score model had the Rams by about 15. Two Hurts →
Darius Cooper touchdowns and two Jake Elliott field goals built the lead; two Kyren Williams scores in
the last four minutes took it away.

**Jalen Hurts** went **12-of-27 for 93 yards**, 2 TD, 0 INT, and was sacked four times (78.2 passer
rating, 37.6 QBR). His PFF grade of **36.9 was last of 33 Week 4 QBs**, a week after a 38.0 at
Chicago. The interior line was a big part of it (LG Cam Jurgens 28.3, C Drew Kendall 43.6), and so was
the missing talent. The defense did its part: **Cooper DeJean** and **Andrew Mukuba** intercepted
Stafford, and 415 yards produced only 24 points.

**Injuries and departures** (depth string: original slot → effective slot):
- **RB Saquon Barkley** (**right hamstring**) — **Starter → hurt on his second carry**, **week-to-week**.
  It's not considered long-term, but he's in serious doubt for Week 5 against Jacksonville in London.
- **RB Tank Bigsby** (**core muscle**) — **2nd string → effective starter (graded 80.5) → IR.** He needs
  **sports-hernia surgery** and is out about 6–8 weeks. **Will Shipley** is now the **3rd string →
  effective starter.**
- **CB Quinyon Mitchell** (**knee**, hyperextension) — **Starter → left the game.** The team hopes it's
  a bone bruise, and Vic Fangio is "hopeful" he plays Week 5.
- **The offensive line came apart off the field this week.** **RT Lane Johnson** (Starter, five-time
  All-Pro) **retired on Oct. 6**, citing his mental health. **Markel Bell** (2nd string, backup LT)
  had already been filling in at right tackle (36.6 pass-block grade in Week 4) and is now the
  **effective starter for good**. A day later, Philadelphia **traded Pro Bowl C Cam Jurgens to
  Baltimore** for draft picks. Jurgens had been playing left guard with **Landon Dickerson**
  (Starter) out. That leaves **Drew Kendall** (2nd string C → effective starter) at center and
  **Willie Lampkin** (2nd string LG → projected effective starter) at left guard until Dickerson
  returns. Mailata and Tyler Steen are the only original starters left.
- Good news: **TE Dallas Goedert** and **LB Zack Baun** are back at practice.

**What moved the number:**
- **QB Hurts +2.0 → +1.5 (−0.5):** two straight games at the bottom of the league. The supporting cast
  explains some of it, but not 93 yards on 27 attempts. His first two games (75.6 and 80.2) keep this
  at a half-point, not more.
- **Offense +0.2 → −1.0 (−1.2):** the biggest move on the board this week. The line came apart in two
  days: Lane Johnson retired and Cam Jurgens was traded, so only two of five starters are left, with
  backups at RT, C and LG. On top of that, the top two running backs are out (Saquon's hamstring,
  Bigsby's surgery), and the receiving corps was already thin. A line that's backups at three spots in
  front of a struggling quarterback, with the third-string back carrying the ball, is a below-average
  offense. Goedert, Dickerson and Saquon returning are the levers back.
- **Defense held at 0.0:** it bent but didn't break, with two takeaways. Mitchell's knee and Baun's
  return roughly cancel out.
- **New total +2.2 → +0.5.**

## Week 3: at Chicago (L 7–27)

Philadelphia got **thrashed on Monday night by a third-string quarterback**. The box says it was
every bit of 20: Chicago out-gained the Eagles 375–248, won the turnover battle 3–0 and held the
ball for almost 37 minutes, and Philadelphia managed **52 yards and no points in the second half**.
**Jalen Hurts** had his worst game of the year (16-of-25, 153, 0 TD, 1 INT; 38.0 PFF, 41.2 QBR),
holding the ball and taking pressure on nearly half his dropbacks. He was pulled for a concussion
evaluation after a late hit and **cleared**; Andy Dalton threw an end-zone interception in the four
plays he was out. **Jonathan Greenard** (pectoral) made his season debut and graded 91 on 18 snaps.

**Injuries:** S **Andrew Mukuba** is playing through a knee issue (not 100%). Already out: TE
Dallas Goedert (MCL sprain, avoided IR), WR Hollywood Brown (ankle), T Fred Johnson (knee), G
Landon Dickerson (IR, knee).

**What changed:**
- **Defense +0.5 → 0.0 (−0.5):** Case Keenum, in his first start, went 24-of-34 for 247 and two
  touchdowns **without being sacked**, and Chicago scored 17 unanswered after halftime. CB Tariq
  Woolen (40) and Mukuba (35) struggled. Greenard's return is the upside.
- **QB Hurts held at +2.0:** one bad night after two good weeks (75.6 and 80.2 PFF).
- **Offense held at +0.2:** the missing receivers were already priced; the line graded well and
  Barkley ran for 5.5 a carry.
- **New total 2.7 → 2.2.**

## Week 2: at Tennessee (W 24–20)

Unimpressive again, but Philadelphia **keeps finding ways to win** — Jalen Hurts hit Darius
Cooper for a 3-yard touchdown with **nine seconds left** to steal one in 157°F Nashville heat.
Hurts graded well (80.2) despite two interceptions, and **DeVonta Smith (93.2) carried the
offense** with A.J. Brown out. Tennessee "won the tape" (led late, out-gained Philly in the
second half) but couldn't close.

**What moved:** **Offense +0.5 → +0.2.** TE **Dallas Goedert** left with a knee injury (out a few
weeks) — the No. 2 receiving option gone behind an already Brown-less group. QB and Defense
held; the result doesn't move the overall grade. **Watch:** Saquon Barkley took a stinger on the
opening play (returned, precautionary MRI) — if he's limited going forward, another small offense
trim.

## Week 1: vs Washington (W 24–22)

Philadelphia opened 1–0 but had to hang on, 24–22. Jalen Hurts was efficient (14-of-25, 203,
three touchdowns plus 46 rushing yards, including a 22-yard scramble on 3rd-and-18) and Dallas
Goedert caught two scores, but the offense had stretches of trouble and Washington's front
sacked Hurts three times before a late Commanders rally fell a two-point try short. The rating
holds; the one new concern is up front — **guard Landon Dickerson went to IR (knee)**, a real
dent to a line that had been a clear strength.

## The case for above average

Philadelphia grades above the line — a proven quarterback, a talented supporting cast, and a defense holding up despite edge losses.

## Quarterback

Jalen Hurts (+1.5) is an above-average dual-threat grade, cut from +2.0 after Week 4. Two straight bottom-of-the-league games (38.0 and 36.9 PFF) were partly about a depleted cast, but they still move the number. The rushing value is baked in.

## What moved the number

- **Offense (+0.5):** raised from 0.0. Even after **losing A.J. Brown**, this is a strong group: **Saquon Barkley is an elite back**, DeVonta Smith headlines a still-solid receiver room, Dallas Goedert is a real tight end, and the offensive line is very good (Mailata, Dickerson, Lane Johnson). Barkley plus that line is enough to grade above average on its own.
- **Defense (0.0; cut from +0.5 after Week 3):** the secondary is a young strength (Quinyon Mitchell, DeJean, Woolen, Mukuba) and the interior is stout, but they lost edge production (Graham, Ojulari, Phillips) and added Greenard and Epenesa — the pass rush is the question that keeps this from grading higher.

## Bottom line

A balanced, above-average roster with a quarterback who wins. Losing A.J. Brown trims the ceiling, but Barkley and the line keep the offense above the line; the edge rush is the one thing to watch.
