## Week 5: at Tennessee (W 26–3)

Houston's first win was never in doubt. The Texans led 12–3 at halftime on four Ka'imi Fairbairn field
goals, then put it away with a **C.J. Stroud 2-yard TD run** and a **Stroud → Jaylin Noel 9-yard TD**.
The defense held Tennessee to **187 yards (3.2 per play)**, sacked Cam Ward four times and forced two
turnovers. **Danielle Hunter** had 10 pressures and **Will Anderson Jr.** had 3 sacks. The box-score
model had it about 25–10, so this was earned. The one complaint is the red zone: Houston settled for
field goals on 2 of 4 trips, and committed 11 penalties again.

**Stroud** went 26-of-34 for 261 yards with a TD (and a rushing TD), a 107.6 passer rating, a 63.0
QBR and a **79.9 PFF pass grade, tied for 3rd of 30 QBs**. That's his second straight week near the
top of the league, and he **still hasn't thrown an interception this season**. RG Ed Ingram bounced
back from a rough Week 4 with an 89.0 grade.

**Injuries** (depth string: original slot → effective slot):
- **S/nickel Jalen Pitre** (**shoulder, AC joint**) — **Starter → left the game, week-to-week.**
  **Kamari Ramsey** goes **2nd string → effective starter**.
- **RB David Montgomery** (**groin**) — **2nd string → left in the 2nd quarter, week-to-week.**
  Woody Marks stays the lead back.
- **LB Henry To'oTo'o** (shoulder, IR) and **LB E.J. Speed** (quad, PUP) — **Starters → still out.**
  **Jake Hansen** (2nd string → effective starter) graded 86.4.

**What moved the number:**
- **QB Stroud +0.5 → +1.0 (+0.5):** two straight weeks near the top of the league by PFF and no
  interceptions in five games. Protection has been his governor, not his play.
- **Offense +0.3 → +0.5 (+0.2):** **Marvin Harrison Jr.** arrives from Arizona (traded Oct. 11 for a
  2027 2nd-round pick) and gives Nico Collins a real No. 2 receiver (+0.3, modest while he learns the
  playbook mid-season). Montgomery's groin takes away the change-of-pace back (−0.1).
- **Defense held at +1.5:** dominant, but against one of the league's worst offenses, and Pitre is
  now week-to-week. Those offset.
- **New total +2.3 → +3.0.**

## Week 4: vs Dallas (L 30–34)

Houston is **0–4**, and this was the most painful loss yet: a **16–6 halftime lead** blown at home, then
a go-ahead Woody Marks touchdown with 2:23 left undone by a Dak → CeeDee Lamb TD at 0:40. The box-score
model had Dallas by about 5, so this wasn't bad luck. The yardage was even (399–397) and neither side
turned it over, but Dallas ran **28 first downs to Houston's 16**. The Texans also beat themselves with
**13 penalties for 90 yards** and went 3-of-10 on third down.

The good news is the quarterback. With **Nico Collins back** (7 catches, 118 yards, 2 TD), **C.J. Stroud
had his best game of the year**: 21-of-31, 347 yards, 2 TD, 0 INT, a **126.7 passer rating** and a
**91.8 PFF grade, best of 33 Week 4 QBs**. His QBR (54.8) was lower because the offense ran only 55
snaps. The line remains the problem: RG Ed Ingram (38.6), C Evan Brown and LT Aireontae Ersery all
graded poorly.

**Injuries** (depth string: original slot → effective slot):
- **CB Jaylin Smith** (**hamstring**) — **2nd string → left in the 2nd quarter → IR.**
- **DT Jaden Crumedy** (**shoulder**) — **2nd string (rotational) → hurt**, status unclear.
- **LB Henry To'oTo'o** (**shoulder**, IR) and **LB E.J. Speed** (**quad**, PUP) — **Starters → still
  out.** **Jake Hansen** and **Kenneth Murray** are **2nd string → effective starters**, so the
  linebacker room is a full layer deep into its depth chart.

**What moved the number:**
- **Defense +2.0 → +1.5 (−0.5):** a full half-point. The strength of this team gave up **34 points and
  28 first downs** to a Dallas offense missing its All-Pro guard, and it blew a 16–6 halftime lead at
  home. Will Anderson Jr. (86.0, 7 pressures) is still elite, but the coverage behind Derek Stingley
  graded poorly (Lassiter 49.4, Bullock 49.6), and both starting linebackers are out, with backups
  playing every down. It's still an above-average unit, but not a +2.0 one right now.
- **QB Stroud held at +0.5:** the best-graded game of the week, with the wins blocked by what's around
  him rather than by him. If he keeps grading like this, there's room above his number.
- **Offense held at +0.3:** Nico's return (+1.0) was already applied before kickoff.
- **New total +2.8 → +2.3.**

## Week 3: at Indianapolis (L 17–19)

Houston dropped to 0–3 on a 53-yard field goal with 16 seconds left, and it's hard to lose a
game like this. The Texans **won the turnover battle 3–0**, got another dominant game from
**Will Anderson Jr.** (a 93 PFF grade, 2.5 sacks), and still lost, because the offense
couldn't move without Nico Collins: 223 yards, 4.4 per play, 4-of-11 on third down. **C.J.
Stroud** was clean (16-of-27, 167, 1 TD, 0 INT, a 75.7 PFF grade) but had nobody to push the
ball to. Woody Marks' 1-yard touchdown put Houston up 17–16 with 1:48 left, and DeMeco Ryans
took the blame for the late-game timeout management. The expected-score model actually had
Houston winning narrowly. This one was a coin flip decided by the offense's lack of
firepower, not a talent verdict.

## Week 1: vs Buffalo (L 31–36)

Houston lost a home shootout, 31–36, and it split along the rating's fault lines. The elite
defense generated relentless pressure — eight sacks and twelve hits on Josh Allen — but
couldn't finish, surrendering 36 to an MVP in vintage form (quality of competition, not a
collapse). The offense hung 31 with David Montgomery a bright spot, but C.J. Stroud struggled
again, reinforcing the prove-it read. The rating holds: giving up points to peak Allen doesn't
indict the defense — which validated its identity with the pressure it generated — and Stroud's
inconsistency is already priced into the low quarterback grade.

## The case for a defense-led team

Houston's grade is built on one of the best defenses in football, with an ascending-but-unproven-in-2025 quarterback and an offense whose ceiling is capped by protection more than talent.

## Quarterback

C.J. Stroud (+1.0) is up a half-point after two straight weeks near the top of the league (91.8 PFF
in Week 4, 1st of 33; 79.9 pass grade in Week 5, tied for 3rd of 30). He has **no interceptions
through five games**. The arm talent and poise were never the question. The protection was, and he's
producing behind a line that still has holes. There's more room above this number if it continues.

## What moved the number

- **Offense (+0.3):** **Nico Collins is a legitimate WR1**, and the run game got a boost, but the supporting cast is thin and banged up — **Tank Dell and Jayden Higgins are both on IR**, and the free-agent addition (Boutte) is still learning the playbook. The real governor is up front: the offensive line has been the problem, and with **Braden Smith on IR** the protection is a live concern in a division that gets after the quarterback. Good top-end skill, not enough around it — a slight positive.
- **Defense (+2.0):** the identity, and an **elite grade**. **Will Anderson and Danielle Hunter are arguably the best edge pair in the NFL**, Derek Stingley Jr. and Kamari Lassiter give it a shutdown perimeter, and Jalen Pitre patrols the back. Crucially, the unit is **essentially fully healthy** heading into Week 1 — Jadeveon Clowney (hip/ankle) is the only ding and is expected to play. This is a top-tier, win-games-by-itself defense.

## Bottom line

A defense good enough to carry the team on its own, plus a quarterback the model still believes in. The whole question is the offensive line: keep Stroud upright and this is a dangerous team; leave him exposed and the offense stays a tick above average while the defense does the heavy lifting.
