## Week 2: at Atlanta (W 34–3)

Carolina bounced back emphatically, 34–3 at Atlanta — complementary football. Bryce Young was
clean and efficient (287 yards, three touchdowns, no picks, PFF 79), Chuba Hubbard rolled
behind a strong day up front (center Ryan Neuzil graded 92.8), and the **defense pitched a
near-shutout while forcing four-plus turnovers** — Devin Lloyd (92.3) everywhere, Jaycee Horn
locking his side.

The caveat is the opponent: **Atlanta was down to third-string Cooper Rush** — Penix (ACL) and
Tua (oblique) both out — and Rush was awful (PFF 26.7, two interceptions including a pick-six).
So the blowout is real but quality-of-competition capped. **Injury:** safety Nick Scott (ribs)
left in the first quarter and was ruled out — a depth loss, covered in relief.

**What moved:** Offense −0.8 → −0.6 (+0.2) and Defense −0.5 → −0.2 (+0.3), both kept
deliberately small for the soft opponent. The defense earns the larger bump for proving it can
dominate a bad quarterback and shut down the opponent's top weapons; Young held steady.

## Week 1: vs Chicago (L 37–59)

Carolina lost a track meet, 37–59, on the wrong end of the highest-scoring opener in NFL
history — and the takeaways split cleanly by side of the ball. The **defense was humiliated**,
allowing the most points in franchise history. But the **offense was a genuine bright spot**:
**Bryce Young threw for 361 yards and three touchdowns and ran for another** — competing in a
shootout **behind a line missing both starting tackles** (Ekwonu and Moton), with Jalen Coker
going for 138 and two scores. Young earns a real bump for that; the defense takes the hit.

## The case: a rising QB, a leaky defense

Carolina still grades below the line, but the weight has shifted — the quarterback and offense
are trending up, while the defense is now the drag.

## Quarterback

Bryce Young (+0.5 → +1.0) climbs after the best kind of Week 1: 361 yards, three touchdown
passes and a rushing score while playing behind a patchwork line. The model has warmed to him,
and putting up that line without his tackles is exactly the evidence it wanted.

## What moved the number

- **Offense (now −0.6):** nudged up twice — 37 points and 361 passing yards in Wk1 **without
  both starting tackles**, then +0.2 after the Atlanta win. Climbs further as Ekwonu and Moton
  return.
- **Defense (now −0.2):** cut to −0.5 after a franchise-record point total in Wk1, then **+0.3
  after Atlanta** — a near-shutout with four-plus takeaways that showed the unit (Jaelan
  Phillips, Devin Lloyd) can dominate a bad quarterback and smother an opponent's top weapons.
  Held small because Atlanta was without its top two QBs.

## Risk

The arrow on offense and quarterback points up, especially once the line is whole; the
defense is now the question. DT Bobby Brown (back) is a short-term watch.
