## Week 1: at Detroit (L 30–31, OT)

New Orleans lost a 31–30 overtime heartbreaker at Detroit, but the story was the fight:
down 21–0, second-year QB Tyler Shough — playing **without Alvin Kamara** (knee, inactive)
**and rookie first-round WR Jordyn Tyson** — led the Saints all the way back to force
overtime, where they fell on a failed two-point conversion. A genuinely encouraging look
from the quarterback given who was missing, tempered by the fact it came from behind and
still ended in a loss.

## The case for a below-average grade

New Orleans grades below the line — an ascending second-year quarterback with a defense that
keeps thinning out, though the skill group keeps the offense afloat.

## Quarterback

Tyler Shough (−0.5) is a below-average but **ascending second-year** grade (not a rookie).
The Week 1 comeback — 21 points down, without Kamara or Tyson — showed more NFL capability
than his prior replacement-level price implied, so the number ticks up. Still below the line,
with room to keep climbing if he backs it up.

## What moved the number

- **Quarterback (−1.0 → −0.5):** the 21-point comeback, shorthanded, earned a half-point
  bump — a real signal on Shough, held short of average because it came from behind in a loss.
- **Offense (0.0):** Chris Olave (WR1) and Alvin Kamara anchor it, with Etienne added — but
  **Kamara** missed Week 1 with a knee injury and **rookie WR Jordyn Tyson is out ~2 months**,
  so the group is playing shorthanded around a second-year quarterback.
- **Defense (−0.3):** cut from 0.0. The unit was already thin (lost Davis), and the IR sheet has piled on — **DT Bryan Bresee, LB Jaylan Ford (season-ending), and CB David Long** — with Werner, Riley, and Jordan all questionable. Chase Young and Cameron Jordan off the edge are the bright spot keeping it near replacement level.

## Bottom line

The skill group keeps the offense respectable, but a still-developing second-year quarterback and a defense losing bodies weekly hold this below the line — though Shough's Week 1 fight is a reason the arrow is pointing up.
