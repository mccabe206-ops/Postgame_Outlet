## The case for a defense-carried rebuild

The Giants grade a shade below the line — a rookie quarterback and a thin, aging skill group weighing down what is genuinely the strength of this roster: the pass rush.

## Quarterback

Jaxson Dart (-1.0) is a Day-1 rookie starter, and -1.0 fits the profile cleanly: above the worst-starter floor, below a proven middling veteran. There's developmental upside, but the rating is priced for the growing pains of a first-year passer without much around him.

## What moved the number

- **Offense (-0.5):** **Malik Nabers is a legitimate WR1** — the one real difference-maker — but he's working back from a 2025 torn ACL, so even though he's expected to play, he may not be 100% early. Behind him the room is aging and uninspiring (OBJ, Mooney, JuJu), and it's all in front of a rookie QB. Below average, and the Nabers recovery is the reason not to bump it up.
- **Defense (+0.5):** the best unit on the team by a distance. Reporting frames the **edge trio — Brian Burns, Abdul Carter, and Kayvon Thibodeaux — as one of the most dangerous front-end groups in the league**, with DJ Reader anchoring the interior. What caps the grade is the back end: the secondary is thin and dinged (Greg Newsome and Dru Phillips both banged up), so an elite rush is paired with a shaky coverage group.

## Bottom line

A fearsome front and a rookie's upside are the reasons for optimism; a recovering WR1, an aging supporting cast, and a thin secondary are why the overall number sits just below average. If the pass rush travels and Nabers gets to full speed, this climbs — the QB spot is what it's waiting on.
